How to choose a pet urn size

Use capacity as a starting point, then confirm the details with the crematory and in your written preview.

By MadeMem · Last updated November 11, 2026

Begin with a planning guideline

Allow about 1 cubic inch of capacity per 1 pound of healthy body weight. For a pet whose healthy weight was 30 pounds, about 30 cubic inches is a planning estimate.

The crematory can help you understand the volume you will receive. Ask about any container or bag that will stay inside the urn, since that affects the space you need.

Confirm the interior capacity

Outside dimensions alone do not tell you the usable capacity. Discuss the interior volume and the opening before choosing an urn. Let us know if you want room for a small keepsake.

Typical size — confirmed in your preview. We confirm the size in writing before making. If that confirmed size is wrong, we remake it at no charge.

Keep pet and human urn choices separate

A pet urn is selected for the pet and the space needed. Full-size human urns in our human memorial collection hold 200 cubic inches or more.

Tell us which type you need in your inquiry. We will confirm the intended use and capacity in the preview.

Fill at home when you are ready

You fill standard urns and vials yourself at home. Standard products do not require mailing ashes or hair. Work on a stable surface with a clean paper underneath. Follow the opening and closure guidance confirmed for your piece. If you would prefer help, ask your crematory. For a vial, confirm its seal and closure before wearing it. Special custom plans — contact us.

Keep any supplied closure guidance with the piece. For a necklace vial, check its seal and closure before wearing it. If you would prefer assistance, ask your crematory about local help.

Give yourself time to decide

You can choose a shelf or quiet corner before settling on the design. Consider how the opening will be reached and whether other family members want smaller sharing pieces.

Burial and scattering rules vary by location. Check the relevant local requirements before making those plans. Your crematory may be able to point you toward the appropriate local information.

When you feel ready, you can ask a question or explore a piece at your own pace.
